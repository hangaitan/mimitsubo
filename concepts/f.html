What is mimitsubo?

about.txt×

Your ear is a tiny map. For centuries, East Asian traditions have pressed points on the ear that are linked to different parts of the body. In the 1950s a French doctor, Paul Nogier, drew the ear map the West still uses today.

In Japan the idea became something playful: small seeds or 24k gold pellets on a clear patch, worn like ear jewellery and pressed a few times a day.

How to use

  1. lv.1

    Clean

    Wipe your ear with the cleansing wipe and let it dry.

  2. lv.2

    Find

    Move the copper pen slowly over the area until you find the point you want.

  3. lv.3

    Place

    Lift one patch and press it onto the point, seed against the skin.

  4. lv.4

    Wear

    Press each seed softly for about 30 seconds, a few times a day. Take the patch off after three to five days.

FAQ

How long do I wear them?

Three to five days. Then take them off, give your ear a day or two of rest, and switch to the other ear.

How often do I press?

A few times a day, softly, about 30 seconds each time. After coffee, on the bus, before bed. Whenever you want a small moment.

Does it hurt?

No. The seeds only sit on top of the skin, and nothing pierces it. Pressing should feel firm, never sharp.

Who should skip it?

Skip it if you have irritated skin or a cut on your ear. If you are pregnant or under medical care, ask your doctor first. Take a patch off if your skin gets itchy or red.

Is it a medical treatment?

No. Mimitsubo is a self-care ritual and ear jewellery. It doesn't replace advice from a doctor.

Cosmetic accessory, worn on the ear. External use only. Do not place on broken or irritated skin, and take the patch off if it bothers you. Keep out of reach of children.
