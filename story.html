What is mimitsubo?

A short history · 3 min read

Your ear is a tiny map. For centuries, East Asian traditions have pressed points on the ear that are linked to different parts of the body. In the 1950s a French doctor, Paul Nogier, drew the ear map the West still uses today.

Nogier's own story began with a folk remedy. He noticed that several of his patients in rural France carried the same small mark on the same fold of the ear — left there by a local healer who treated sciatica by cauterising that one spot. Curious, he began mapping the ear point by point, matching each region to a part of the body, until the whole outer ear resembled a tiny, curled figure: head at the lobe, spine along the ridge.

The idea travelled east and changed shape. In China and Korea it folded into older traditions of ear reflexology that already linked the ear to the body's organs. In Japan it became something gentler and more everyday — not a clinic visit, but a small habit. A tiny seed, or later a dot of 24k gold, replaced the needle. It sits on a clear patch, worn like a fleck of jewellery, and is pressed rather than pierced.

An old idea, dressed for every day.

That's the version Mammi is built around — no needles, no clinic, nothing pierced. Just a small patch, a point you find yourself, and a few quiet seconds of pressing it, as often or as rarely as the day allows.
